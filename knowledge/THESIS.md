# Thesis — Narrow Center / Living Edge

## The Core Thesis

Agent governance will become a product category because organizations are delegating institutional authority to software faster than policy, compliance, and platform teams can absorb. The winning product will not be a generic AI safety dashboard. It will be an operating layer that lets teams encode a narrow set of invariant rails while preserving local experimentation at the edge. Narrow Center / Living Edge can win by turning a hard governance philosophy into concrete product artifacts: classifications, compacts, capability maps, scenario decisions, and audit trails.

## Market Opportunity

### Total Addressable Market

Every enterprise team deploying agents with real permissions: developer platforms, customer support, procurement, finance operations, commerce, payments, risk, legal ops, and internal productivity.

### Serviceable Market

Organizations running multi-agent or multi-team AI systems where agents can access private data, call tools, spend money, message users, or mutate production-adjacent assets.

### Initial Target

Platform and strategy teams designing agentic commerce, payment-agent flows, or internal agent fleets with budget/tool authority.

## Competitive Landscape

### Direct Competitors

| Competitor | What They Do | How Narrow Center / Living Edge Is Different |
|------------|--------------|----------------------------------------------|
| AI governance platforms | Policy, risk registers, model inventories, compliance workflows | Product-level operating compacts and runtime capability design for agent fleets. |
| Agent observability tools | Logs, traces, evals, run analytics | Begins before runtime: classify powers, author charters, shape authority, and connect traceability to governance doctrine. |
| Access-control / IAM products | Credentials, permissions, role-based access | Treats authority as institutional context: purpose, budget, provenance, escalation, and revocation. |
| Agent frameworks | Build and orchestrate agents | Gives the governance grammar frameworks usually leave to prompts and docs. |

### Indirect Competitors

| Alternative | Why People Use It | Why Narrow Center / Living Edge Wins |
|-------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Policy documents | Familiar, low-cost, easy to approve | They do not become operating artifacts or runtime design constraints. |
| Manual review boards | Legible accountability | They become bottlenecks and push teams into informal workarounds. |
| One-off prompt rules | Fast for prototypes | They are brittle, unauditable, and difficult to revoke consistently. |

## Moats & Defensibility

### What makes it hard to copy

- A distinct governance grammar: center / edge / observe / forbid.
- Compacts as structured artifacts rather than prose policies.
- Fleet-level institutional memory: what powers exist, who granted them, why, and when they should dissolve.
- Scenario history that becomes a reusable body of governance precedent.
- A brand and doctrine that feels institutional rather than SaaS-generic.

### What gets stronger with scale

- More agent charters create better defaults and anti-pattern detection.
- Scenario decisions become precedent libraries.
- Audit trails become governance memory.
- Cross-team adoption creates a shared language for agent authority.

## Business Model

### How It Makes Money

- Team/workspace subscription for governance workbench.
- Enterprise plan for SSO, audit exports, retention controls, and platform integrations.
- Advisory / implementation package for high-stakes agent fleet design.
- Future API for embedding classification and compact validation into agent CI/CD.

### Unit Economics

| Metric | Early Assumption |
|--------|------------------|
| CAC | Founder-led / strategic wedge through agentic-commerce and AI governance teams. |
| LTV | High if embedded into platform approval and audit workflows. |
| Payback | Fast for design-partner motion; longer for enterprise platform sale. |

### Path to Profitability

Start as a focused workbench with a few design partners. Convert repeatable governance artifacts into productized workflows. Expand into integrations with agent frameworks, observability stacks, and enterprise identity/access systems.

## Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| Category feels too philosophical | Medium | High | Ship concrete tools first: inventory, scenario simulator, exports, tests. |
| Governance buyers want compliance checklists | Medium | Medium | Map outputs to audit needs without becoming checklist software. |
| Agent frameworks add similar primitives | High | Medium | Stay framework-agnostic and own cross-fleet institutional memory. |
| Product becomes another dashboard | Medium | High | Keep the unit of value as generated operating artifacts and decisions. |
| Too much centralization creeps in | High | High | Make edge freedom a measured product primitive, not a slogan. |
