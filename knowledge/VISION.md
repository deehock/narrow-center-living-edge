# Vision — Narrow Center / Living Edge

## The One-Liner

Narrow Center / Living Edge is a governance workbench for teams operating AI agent fleets: decide what belongs in invariant rails, let the edge adapt, and keep every agent accountable.

## The Problem

Agent systems are being governed in two bad ways at once. One instinct centralizes everything into a permission bureaucracy that kills local judgment. The other hands agents credentials, memory, tools, voice, and budget, then hopes policy prose will behave like infrastructure.

The teams feeling this first are platform operators, AI governance owners, developer-platform teams, and payments/commerce builders whose agents can spend money, speak to humans, access private context, or delegate work.

## The Solution

Build a product workbench that turns chaordic governance into operating artifacts:

- classify a proposed agent capability as **center**, **edge**, **observe**, or **forbid**;
- author compact-style charters for each agent;
- maintain a fleet inventory with powers, budgets, escalation paths, and revocation state;
- simulate governance scenarios before credentials go live;
- produce audit-ready doctrine, not generic policy decks.

## Why This Works

The product starts from a simpler design question: what must be identical everywhere for trust to exist, and what must vary locally for intelligence to emerge? That is the original network-association trick. Settlement, identity, authority, audit, budget, and revocation belong at the center. Voice, tactics, experiments, partner workflows, and channel behavior belong at the edge, bounded by a compact.

## The Shift

**Before:** agent governance is a mixture of static policy documents, scattered prompts, manual reviews, ad hoc allowlists, and post-hoc logs.

**After:** every agent carries a compact; every capability has a governance zone; every material action is tied to authority, budget, provenance, and revocation; every edge experiment has a visible learning loop.

## Why Now

Agents are moving from chat interfaces into work: spending, scheduling, messaging, code mutation, sales, support, procurement, payment flows, and cross-agent delegation. The old distinction between “software behavior” and “institutional authority” is collapsing. That makes governance a product problem, not a compliance appendix.

## The End State

In three to five years, serious organizations will not ask whether they “use agents.” They will ask whether their agent fleets have credible operating compacts, revocable authority, bounded budgets, auditable memory, and enough freedom at the edge to learn. Narrow Center / Living Edge should become the practical instrument for designing that balance.
