# Narrative — Narrow Center / Living Edge

## The Narrative Stack

### 1. Tagline

Govern fewer things absolutely. Observe many things continuously.

### 2. Tweet

AI agent fleets need the Visa pattern: a narrow center for identity, authority, budget, audit, settlement, and revocation — and a living edge for voice, tactics, tools, and experiments. Governance should be a workbench, not a PDF.

### 3. Elevator Pitch

Most teams are about to give agents real authority with fake governance. Narrow Center / Living Edge gives them a way to design the compact first: what must be invariant, what can vary locally, what should only be observed, and what must be forbidden at the rail. The output is not a deck. It is a fleet map, charters, scenario decisions, and an audit trail.

### 4. One-Pager

AI agents are becoming institutional actors. They can speak in a company’s voice, use tools, remember context, spend budget, mutate code, message customers, and delegate to other agents. Existing governance patterns are too soft or too rigid: policy documents do not constrain runtime behavior, and centralized approval queues do not scale at machine speed.

Narrow Center / Living Edge is a product workbench for designing governance before the fleet runs. It helps a team classify capabilities, author compact-style agent charters, inventory powers and limits, simulate risky scenarios, and generate an audit-ready operating doctrine.

The principle is simple: the center must be narrow enough to trust and the edge must be alive enough to learn. Identity, authority, budget, audit, settlement, and revocation are center rails. Voice, tactics, experiments, partner workflows, and local tool composition belong at the edge inside a visible compact.

### 5. Full Story

The first error in agent governance is treating an agent like a program. The second is treating it like an employee. It is neither. It is delegated capability moving through institutional space.

That means the design problem is not “how do we control the agent?” Control is the wrong primitive. The better question is: what agreement makes this agent’s freedom safe enough to be useful?

Visa’s original institutional design solved a similar puzzle. Banks that competed intensely could still cooperate on the rails that had to be identical: authorization, clearing, settlement, marks, rules. The center was narrow. The edge was alive.

Narrow Center / Living Edge applies that pattern to AI agent fleets. It gives operators a product surface for deciding what becomes common law, what remains local judgment, what is watched before it is frozen, and what is forbidden because it attacks trust itself.

## Story Arc

### The Setup

Agents are gaining authority faster than organizations are gaining governance.

### The Shift

The right governance model is not maximum control. It is a narrow invariant center plus a living adaptive edge.

### The Solution

A workbench that turns that model into diagnostics, compacts, fleet inventory, scenario reviews, and audit artifacts.

### The Stakes

Without a compact, agent fleets become either brittle bureaucracies or credentialed chaos.

### The Invitation

Bring one real agent fleet, one risky capability, or one unresolved governance question. Put it through the workbench and see what belongs at the center.

## Audience-Specific Narratives

### For Developers

Stop burying institutional authority in prompts and config flags. Model capabilities as governed resources: identity, scope, budget, provenance, audit, and revocation.

### For Business / Strategy

This is the operating layer for safe agentic growth: enough trust to scale, enough local freedom to discover new workflows.

### For Risk / Governance

This converts policy intent into structured operating artifacts: capability classifications, charters, escalation paths, and audit logs.

### For Platform Teams

A shared language and product surface for approving, monitoring, and retiring agent capabilities across teams without becoming the bottleneck.

## Key Messages

### Must Always Say

- The center must be narrow.
- The edge must be allowed to vary.
- Every agent needs a compact: purpose, powers, limits, reciprocity, dissolution.
- Revocation is not an emergency feature; it is a first-class governance primitive.

### Never Say

- “Autonomous agents can self-govern.”
- “One central agent should approve every action.”
- “This replaces compliance.”
- “Governance is just prompt engineering.”

### Proof Points

- The current app already includes classification, rails, and compact generation.
- The product roadmap extends those primitives into fleet inventory, scenario simulation, and audit exports.
- The governing analogy has already worked at institutional scale: narrow shared rails, competitive/adaptive edge.

## Analogies That Work

| Concept | Analogy |
|---------|---------|
| Narrow center | Authorization, clearing, settlement rails: boring because they must be trusted. |
| Living edge | Issuer/merchant/customer/channel variation: where adaptation happens. |
| Agent compact | A charter plus credential envelope plus audit obligation. |

## Objections & Responses

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| “Why not just use an allowlist?” | Allowlists describe tools, not authority. The compact explains purpose, scope, budget, escalation, and revocation. |
| “Isn’t this compliance software?” | It may serve compliance, but it starts as an operating workbench for builders and platform teams. |
| “Why not centralize every risky action?” | Because risk does not vanish in a queue. It often reappears as shadow systems and slow judgment. Centralize only what must be invariant. |
